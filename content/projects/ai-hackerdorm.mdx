## Context

Two years before we started, I knew nothing about tech. What changed wasn't a course — it was people dragging me into rooms I had no business being in. AI Hackerdorm exists to manufacture that accident for other students, on purpose and at scale.

## The problem

Students who want to build hit the same wall: no access to industry, no peers to build with, and no room where trying something ambitious is normal. The talent is there. The environment isn't.

## Role

I co-founded AI Hackerdorm and serve as vice-president — the builder-ecosystem side: chapter growth, industry partnerships, and the events that put students in front of real builders.

## Process

We started at Taylor's, proved the model, then grew to four chapters nationwide. Growth wasn't the goal in itself — each chapter had to reproduce the *feeling*, not just the logo, so we documented the culture as carefully as the operations.

## Key decisions

- **Students first, industry second.** Every program is designed for the student in the room, then made legible to partners — never the other way around.
- **Make industry accessible, not aspirational.** Active partnership with [AI.SEA](https://aisea.builders) puts students in the same room as working builders from KL to Southeast Asia.
- **Win the right to run big things.** Delivering consistently is what let us co-organise the historic Cursor × Anthropic Hackathon Malaysia 2025.

## Implementation

Today AI Hackerdorm runs four chapters with 700+ members nationwide — build weeks, hackathons, mentorship pipelines. Separately, we co-organised the Cursor × Anthropic Hackathon Malaysia 2025: a $410k USD prize pool that put Malaysian student builders on a regional stage.

## Outcome

A student who walks in knowing nothing about tech can, within a semester, be shipping in a hackathon alongside industry builders. That pipeline — the one I needed and didn't have — now exists.
