## Context

Taylor's Anime Society is one of the university's longest-running student organisations, founded in 2005. I joined as secretary, became vice president, then president — and somewhere in that run I decided the community deserved something bigger than a booth at someone else's convention.

## The problem

Student events usually stay small because they're run on goodwill and group chats. To do something at real scale — real partners, real money, real accountability — you have to build an organisation that behaves like one, without losing the thing that made people show up in the first place.

## Role

I founded KiyokoCon and served as organising chairman: the concept, the budget, the partnership strategy, and the committee structure that made 35 collaborators move in the same direction.

## Process

I ran it like a build. We decomposed the convention into independent workstreams — booths, hackathon, tournaments, TCG events, sponsorships — each with an owner, a number, and a deadline. Then I spent most of my own time on the two things that don't delegate well: relationships and judgment calls under pressure.

## Key decisions

- **Program for adjacent communities, not one.** We paired the anime core with a hackathon by Build Club, tournaments from Insert Coin & Off Stage, and an inter-university TCG event from Quantum Nexus — so the convention pulled five audiences at once.
- **Treat sponsors as partners, not ATMs.** I personally facilitated 11+ key partner and sponsor relationships, which is why 35 total collaborators stayed aligned through the chaos.
- **Build a committee that outlives you.** Roles were documented so the event could run again without me in the room.

## Implementation

KiyokoCon 2025 shipped 50 booths across a full venue, with concurrent programming tracks and 1,700 attendees — visited by founders of tier-one convention organisers in Malaysia, which was its own quiet validation.

## Outcome

KiyokoCon won **Event of the Year 2025, Gold**, from Taylor's Student Development. More durable than the award: a playbook and a team that can run it again.
